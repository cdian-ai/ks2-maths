# The Climb — rebuilt on the papers

Y5–6 maths, Key Stage 2 SATs. Spine, 7 September 2026.

## What this is now based on

Not the programme of study. **The 2025 and 2026 mark schemes** — every question in all six
papers, tagged by the Standards and Testing Agency with the content-domain code it assesses.
**177 questions.** The code descriptions are the official wording from the KS2 mathematics
test framework, not paraphrase.

Two facts worth having before anything else. There are **nine** years of live papers, not ten:
2020 and 2021 were cancelled. And **the 2026 papers are out** — sat this May, published — so
the two years used here are the two most recent tests in existence.

**What this cannot tell you.** Two years is a small sample. 37 of the 85 codes appeared once
only, so the tail is noise. The top of the distribution is solid; whether geometry earns two
stops or three is a guess either way, and is treated as one.

**Nearly a third of the paper is Year 3 and Year 4 content.** The single most-asked code
across both years is **4C6b** — *place value and known facts to multiply and divide mentally* —
a Year 4 objective, asked seven times. A course called "Year 5 and 6 maths" that teaches only
Y5 and Y6 walks past a third of the marks.

## The forty-eight codes asked twice or more

These account for **79% of every question in both papers**. They are the course.

## The climb, rebuilt

Fifty stops, weighted by marks rather than by curriculum. Five camps.

**Camp 1 — The ground you stand on** (8 stops · place value, 11%)
Place value to 10,000,000 · the value of a digit · reading and writing big numbers ·
rounding to any power of ten · negative numbers in context · counting through zero ·
intervals across zero · ordering and comparing.

**Camp 2 — Arithmetic you can trust** (11 stops · calculation, part one)
Times tables to 12 × 12 · **4C6b: multiplying and dividing mentally from what you know**
(the most-asked code in the test — it gets two stops, not one) · column addition and
subtraction to 4 digits and beyond · 2- and 3-digit × 1-digit · short division with
remainders · multiples and factors · primes · squares and cubes · × and ÷ by 10, 100, 1000.

**Camp 3 — The two long methods** (8 stops · calculation, part two)
Long multiplication, four stops: the layout, the tens row, the carry, four digits by two.
Long division, four stops: the algorithm, remainders as whole numbers, remainders as
fractions, four digits by two. **These two carry 9 questions across the two years and
2 marks each — they are the densest marks in the paper.**
Plus order of operations, and multi-step problems in context.

**Camp 4 — Fractions, decimals, percentages** (13 stops · 23%)
Equivalence and simplifying · comparing and ordering, including >1 · adding and
subtracting with different denominators · mixed numbers · multiplying pairs of fractions ·
dividing a fraction by a whole number · fractions of amounts · decimals to three places ·
multiplying decimals by whole numbers · division with decimal answers · the
fraction–decimal–percentage triangle · percentages of amounts · problems to three
decimal places.

**Camp 5 — The rest of the paper** (10 stops · measurement, ratio, statistics, geometry, algebra)
Converting metric units · money and measure problems · perimeter, area of rectangles ·
area of triangles and parallelograms · volume of cuboids · **ratio: relative sizes** and
**unequal sharing** (6R1 and 6R2 together are asked twelve times — more than long
multiplication) · pie charts and line graphs · tables and timetables · angles at a point,
on a line, vertically opposite · co-ordinates, reflection and translation · simple formulae ·
two unknowns · combinations of two variables.

## Three things this changes from the version I built first

**The old unit 1 gave place value nine stops.** It earns eight of fifty, not nine of fifty
when there were only five units planned. Roughly right by accident; wrong in proportion to
everything else, because I had fractions at twelve and calculation at sixteen when the
papers say thirteen and nineteen.

**Y3 and Y4 arithmetic has to be taught, not assumed.** Times tables, column methods and
mental multiplication are a fifth of the paper on their own and they are where a struggling
Year 6 actually loses marks. They are stops, not prerequisites.

**Ratio was going to get four stops. It needs six.** 6R1 and 6R2 are the second and third
most-asked codes in the whole test.

## Still to decide

**How long a stop is.** The first build had two teaching cards and five questions. For
arithmetic that is too few — fluency needs volume — and for the two long methods it is far
too few. I would make calculation stops longer and geometry stops shorter, rather than
holding every stop to the same shape.

**Whether to build a paper.** The obvious end of this course is a timed arithmetic paper —
36 questions, 30 minutes, the real format. That is a different kind of screen from anything
in the climb and it is what the child will actually face.
