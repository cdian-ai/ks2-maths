# Design review — is this actually for children?

The Climb, KS2 maths. 7 September 2026. Reviewed for audience, language and the teaching gap.

## The finding that matters

**74% of the questions in this app are asked without ever being taught.**

| | stops | questions |
|---|---|---|
| Taught \u2014 story, worked example, guided, practice | 11 | 124 |
| Asked with no teaching at all | **36** | **362** |

By camp:

| share of the paper | camp | taught |
|---|---|---|
| 11% | The ground you stand on | 2 of 6 |
| 19% | Arithmetic you can trust | **1 of 11** |
| 14% | The two long methods | 4 of 8 |
| 23% | Fractions, decimals, percentages | 4 of 12 |
| 33% | The rest of the paper | **0 of 10** |

**A third of the marks sit in a camp where nothing whatsoever is taught.** Ratio, area, volume, angles, the mean, formulae, two unknowns \u2014 all of them open straight onto a question.

This is not a shortfall in polish. It is the product failing at the thing it claims to do. A child who could already do short division does not need this app; a child who could not is shown a division and a keypad and left there. The stops most affected are exactly the ones a ten-year-old cannot reason their way into unaided:

*Short division \u00b7 What is left over \u00b7 Multiplying fractions \u00b7 Dividing by a whole number \u00b7 Which comes first \u00b7 Prime numbers \u00b7 Squares and cubes \u00b7 Sharing in a ratio \u00b7 Missing angles \u00b7 Two unknowns*

**Recommendation:** no stop ships without at least a worked example. The full four phases are expensive to write and not always needed \u2014 *Adding in columns* does not need a story \u2014 but **watch me** is the minimum, and it is the phase that does the teaching. Thirty-six worked examples is the job.

## Who is this app talking to?

I counted every English string on a screen a child can read: **197 of them, and twenty-two mention marks, papers, the exam board or the syllabus.**

- *where the marks are densest*
- *Two marks each in the real paper, and one mark survives a single slip if the method is right*
- *Every topic here was counted out of the 2025 and 2026 papers \u2014 all 177 questions, tagged by the exam board with what they test*
- *Here is where the marks are*
- *ordered by how many marks they carry in the real papers*

Every one of those is written to the parent. They are true, they were the point of the research, and **a ten-year-old should not see any of them.** Telling a child that a topic is worth a lot of marks does not motivate them; it tells them the app's real customer is someone else, and it raises the stakes of an activity that should feel low-stakes.

The same information can be given without the exam framing. *This one comes up a lot* is the child's version of *23% of the paper*. It carries the same guidance and none of the pressure.

**Recommendation:** two registers. Everything the child reads loses the exam vocabulary. Everything about marks, papers and coverage moves behind *How this course was built*, where the adult will find it and the child will not.

## Does it engage a ten-year-old?

Honestly assessed: **the taught stops do, the rest do not.**

Fatou and her mangoes works. The builder and his wall works, and it is the only explanation in the app of why the second row of long multiplication starts with a zero. Those stops have a reason to keep going.

The other thirty-six are a number, a keypad and a dot counter. That is a worksheet with a battery. A child will do eight and stop.

Three specific things:

**There is no reward for finishing anything.** A stop ends on a tent emoji and a score. Nothing accumulates, nothing is collected, nothing changes on the shelf beyond a tick. The diagnostic offers the one genuinely motivating moment in the app \u2014 *here are the four things worth your time* \u2014 and it happens once.

**The story stops when the teaching stops.** Fatou appears for three screens and is never seen again, not even in the practice for her own stop. A character who returns is worth more than a character who is introduced.

**Nothing is ever easy on purpose.** Every question in a stop is the same difficulty. There is no gentle first one, no moment of *oh, I can do this*. For a child who arrived believing they are bad at maths, the first question of every stop is another chance to be wrong.

## Can you carry on where you left off?

**No.** I tested it: finished stops and best scores are saved; **your position inside a stop is not.** Leave four questions into Long division and you return to question one. There is no "carry on" card unless you have run the diagnostic.

For a child doing twenty minutes after school this is the difference between progress and repetition.

**Recommendation:** save the open stop and its phase, and put a *Carry on* card at the top of the shelf. It is a small change and it is the one a returning child notices first.

## What I would do, in order

**1. Write the thirty-six missing worked examples.** Nothing else on this list matters while three quarters of the app asks before it teaches. Start with Camp 5, which has none and carries a third of the marks.

**2. Strip the exam language from every child-facing screen.** An afternoon's work, and it changes who the app appears to be for.

**3. Save the place.** Small, and immediately felt.

**4. Make the first question of every stop an easy one.** One line of code in the generator call, and it changes how a stop feels to begin.

**5. Bring the characters back into the practice.** Fatou's stop should still be Fatou's stop when the numbers are bare.
